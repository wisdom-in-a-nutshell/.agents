# Adi Writing Discipline — Reader-Centred Clarity

This is the editing discipline underneath Adi's voice. It borrows the strongest
content-design ideas from GOV.UK/GDS without turning Adi's writing into a
government house style.

The governing principle is: open it up, do not dumb it down.

Keep the substance, nuance, uncertainty, and personality. Remove only what
makes the idea harder to understand than it needs to be.

## Start from the reader

Before writing, identify what the reader needs to:

- understand
- decide
- feel
- do

Write toward that need rather than merely recording everything the author knows.
This does not mean every personal piece needs a practical instruction. The
reader's need may simply be to see an experience or idea clearly.

## Front-load the useful part

- Put the most important point early in the piece.
- Put the main point of a section early in that section.
- Give each paragraph a clear job.
- Prefer conclusion, reasoning, then background when the order is flexible.
- Delay the point only when story, suspense, or emotional pacing genuinely
  improves the piece.

Do not mechanically repeat the conclusion at every level. Front-loading should
create clarity, not a stack of identical summary sentences.

## Make the writing concrete

- Prefer a name, number, date, action, example, or lived detail over a vague
  abstraction.
- Say who did what. Use active voice unless the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Replace inflated nouns with direct verbs when that makes the sentence clearer.
- Remove phrases such as "a range of", "in terms of", and "going forward" when
  they add no information.
- Keep technical language when it is the precise language the audience needs.
  Explain it rather than replacing it with a less accurate everyday word.

## Keep sentences and paragraphs easy to hold

- Default to one main idea per sentence and one topic per paragraph.
- Split sentences when the reader must untangle several ideas at once.
- Prefer short paragraphs, especially on screens.
- Cut duplication and filler.
- Use the shortest version that preserves the real meaning and rhythm.

These are tendencies, not word-count rules. A longer sentence can stay when it
captures Adi reasoning in real time and remains easy to follow. Sentence
fragments can stay when they improve rhythm.

## Make structure earn its place

- Use descriptive, sentence-case headings when they help the reader scan.
- Use bullets for genuinely parallel ideas and numbered lists for real sequences.
- Use descriptive link text instead of "click here" or "read more".
- Use bold sparingly to aid scanning, not to manufacture importance.
- Do not force a loose personal post into headings or bullets merely because
  structured prose is easier to template.

## Preserve Adi while editing

Do not let clarity rules remove:

- first-person experience
- honest uncertainty and thinking in public
- useful metaphors or analogies
- conversational asides
- deliberate rough edges
- the sentence where the actual feeling lives

Clarity wins over clutter. Adi's voice wins over institutional uniformity.

## Final clarity pass

Ask:

- Does the reader encounter the most useful point early enough?
- Could a smart non-expert understand this on the first read?
- Did I preserve the substance and uncertainty rather than simplify them away?
- Does every paragraph add a new idea, example, or necessary turn?
- Can I replace any vague abstraction with something concrete?
- Can I cut anything else without losing meaning, rhythm, or personality?
- Did the edit make this clearer while still sounding like Adi?
